Veterinary SOAP Note Template

A copy-ready template for any vet visit — plus a filled-in wellness example and variations for sick visits, rechecks, and post-surgery checks.

Vets spend 2–3 hours per day on documentation, and one SOAP note takes 10–15 minutes by hand. A template does not write the note for you — it removes the blank page. You fill in the same four sections every time, in the same order, and nothing important gets forgotten.

Copy the template below into your notes app, print it, or use it in the PawScribe workspace — the templates there are free, with no account.

The blank template

Patient

Patient name: [Patient name]
Species / breed: [Species / breed]
Age: [Age]
Sex / reproductive status: [Sex / status]
Weight: [Weight]
Date: [Date]
Veterinarian: [Your name / initials]

S — Subjective

Chief complaint: what is the main reason for the visit, in the owner’s own words?

History of present illness: onset, duration, progression, what makes it better or worse, treatments already tried at home. Include owner observations.

Relevant history: current medications, diet, environment, recent travel, previous conditions, vaccination status, parasite prevention.

Review of systems: appetite, thirst, urination, defecation, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, diarrhoea, energy, behaviour changes.

O — Objective

Physical exam: nose-to-tail findings, including body condition score (BCS), hydration, mentation, temperature, pulse, respiration. Note normal and abnormal findings for every system.

Diagnostic results: in-house tests, lab results, imaging findings from this visit.

A — Assessment

Problem list: prioritised list of active problems from the Subjective and Objective findings. For a healthy patient: “healthy adult, no active problems.”

Differential diagnoses: possible causes for each problem, in order of likelihood.

Diagnosis: confirmed diagnosis, or the most likely working diagnosis.

P — Plan

Diagnostics planned: tests to confirm or rule out your differentials.

Treatments: medications prescribed or given, treatments performed, procedures planned — with dose, route, frequency, and duration.

Client education: what you told the owner, medication instructions, expected outcomes, warning signs to watch for.

Monitoring / follow-up: recheck schedule and next steps.

Prognosis: overall outlook for the patient.

Filled-in sample: routine dog wellness visit

This is an example note, not a real patient. Names are placeholders.

Patient

Patient name: [Patient name]
Species / breed: canine, Golden Retriever
Age: 5 years
Sex / reproductive status: female, spayed
Weight: 65 lbs
Date: [Date]
Veterinarian: [Your name / initials]

S — Subjective

Chief complaint: routine annual wellness exam.

History: owner reports the dog is doing well at home, no specific concerns. Activity normal for age. No limping, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, diarrhoea, or changes in urination or defecation. Eating and drinking normally.

Relevant history: current on a monthly flea and tick preventative and a monthly heartworm preventative. Vaccinations due today. Eats [diet]. No known allergies. Spayed at 6 months. No significant medical history.

Review of systems: good appetite, normal thirst, no gastrointestinal, respiratory, or urinary signs. Good energy and behaviour.

O — Objective

BCS: 5/9. Hydration normal. Mentation: bright, alert, responsive. Temperature: 101.5°F. Pulse: 100 bpm, strong and regular. Respiration: 24/min.

Head, eyes, ears, nose, throat: ears clean, no discharge or odour; eyes clear; mild dental calculus on canines and premolars; mucous membranes pink, capillary refill time under 2 seconds. Lymph nodes: normal, non-painful. Skin: shiny coat, no hair loss, lesions, or parasites. Heart: regular rhythm, no murmurs. Lungs: clear on both sides, no cough. Abdomen: soft, non-painful, no organ enlargement. Musculoskeletal: strong gait, no lameness, joints supple. Neurological: cranial nerves intact, proprioception normal.

Diagnostics today: heartworm test negative; faecal flotation negative.

A — Assessment

1. Healthy adult canine, annual wellness.
2. Mild dental calculus — early-stage dental disease.

P — Plan

Administered DAPPv vaccine and rabies vaccine. Dispensed one month of flea and tick preventative and one month of heartworm preventative. Discussed dental health: recommended a dental cleaning in the next 6–12 months, with dental chews as an interim measure. Explained the importance of annual wellness, dental care, and ongoing parasite prevention; gave vaccine certificates and vaccine-reaction instructions (watch for lethargy, facial swelling, or vomiting). Recheck in one year for annual wellness. Prognosis excellent with continued preventative care.

How to use this template

  1. Gather the Subjective first. Collect the owner’s story and the patient’s history before you start the exam.
  2. Examine, then write the Objective. Record factual findings only — what you saw, felt, and measured.
  3. Assess. From the S and O findings, list the problems, your differentials, and your working diagnosis.
  4. Plan. Diagnostics, treatments, client education, and follow-up — with doses and a recheck date.
  5. Be concise and specific. Short, professional sentences. Another vet should be able to pick up the case from your note alone.

Variations by visit type

The SOAP structure never changes. What changes is where the detail goes.

Wellness exam

S: routine care, preventative measures, owner concerns about overall health, diet, exercise, and parasite control. O: a complete head-to-tail exam, vital signs, BCS, and routine diagnostics such as a heartworm test and faecal exam. A: usually a healthy status, plus small preventative issues such as dental calculus or weight. P: vaccines, parasite prevention, diet and exercise advice, dental care, and the next preventative visit.

Sick visit

S: a detailed history of the presenting complaint — onset, duration, progression, associated symptoms, treatments tried at home — plus a brief review of other systems. O: a thorough exam of the affected system first, then a general assessment; diagnostics aimed at confirming or ruling out your differentials. A: a full problem list with developed differentials and a provisional diagnosis. P: a specific diagnostic and treatment plan, detailed client education, medication instructions, warning signs, and a clear recheck date.

Recheck / follow-up

S: what has changed since the last visit — improvement or worsening, and whether the owner followed the treatment plan. O: re-examine the affected systems and compare with the previous record; repeat the relevant diagnostics. A: update each problem, judge whether the treatment worked, and revise the problem list. P: adjust the treatment, order further tests if needed, and set the next follow-up.

Post-surgery check

S: recovery at home — activity, appetite, signs of pain, incision care, and any concerns. O: examine the surgical site closely for swelling, discharge, opening, or infection; check pain level, mentation, and vital signs. A: healing progress and whether any post-operative complications are present. P: incision care instructions, suture or staple removal if needed, pain-management adjustments, a gradual return to activity, and the next check.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this SOAP template for cats?

Yes. The S/O/A/P structure is the same for every species. Change the prompts to fit the patient: litter box habits and indoor/outdoor status for cats, for example, instead of walks and dog-park visits.

Do I need all four sections every time?

Yes, even for a routine visit. For a healthy patient the Assessment can be one line — healthy adult, no active problems — and the Plan covers vaccines, parasite prevention, and the next visit.

Can I change the template for my clinic?

Yes. The template is a starting point. Add your clinic’s standard phrases, remove sections you never use, and keep one version per visit type so the whole team writes notes the same way. PawScribe Pro lets you save custom templates in the workspace.

How long does a SOAP note take to write?

One SOAP note takes 10–15 minutes by hand, and vets spend 2–3 hours per day on documentation. A template removes the blank-page problem, and an AI scribe can turn rough notes into an editable SOAP draft in seconds.
