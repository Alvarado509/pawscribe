Every day, veterinarians face the same problem: documentation. Vets spend 2–3 hours per day on it, and a single SOAP note takes 10–15 minutes to write by hand. There are 130,415 veterinarians in the US, and 93.9% of vet practices are independently owned — small teams where every minute of the vet's day counts twice.

The good news: you do not need a new system to get some of that time back. These tactics work today, with the tools you already have.

1. Use templates for every common visit

Build one template per visit type — wellness exam, sick visit, recheck, post-surgery check — with the four SOAP sections and short prompts under each one. Instead of starting from a blank page, you fill in the blanks and delete what does not apply. Notes come out consistent, nothing gets forgotten, and the mental effort drops because the structure is already decided. See the veterinary SOAP note template for a copy-ready starting point, or use the free templates in the PawScribe template library.

2. Dictate instead of typing

Your voice is faster than your fingers. Dictate your notes during the visit or immediately after, while the details are fresh — into your phone, a dictation app, or your computer's built-in dictation. Speaking the findings as you go can speed up the first draft of a note, and it captures the small details you would forget by the end of the day.

3. Write notes during the visit

It feels like multitasking, but jotting key observations down during the exam saves real time later. A few bullet points — the owner's main concern, the abnormal findings, your first impression — give you a solid base to build the note from, instead of reconstructing several cases from memory hours later. It also lets you clarify things with the owner on the spot, while they are still in the room.

4. Use short standard phrases

Agree on a set of standard abbreviations and short phrases for findings you write every day, and use them the same way across the whole practice. "Bright, alert, and responsive" becomes "BAR." "Within normal limits" becomes "WNL." A shared shorthand is quick to write and quick for the next person to read — as long as everyone in the practice means the same thing by it.

5. Let an AI scribe write the first draft

An AI scribe turns your rough notes, a dictated summary, or a recorded consultation into an editable SOAP draft in seconds, already sorted into Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan. You start from a structured draft instead of a blank page. PawScribe's free plan includes 5 AI notes a month, and the templates and note builder are free with no account.

An honest note on AI

An AI scribe saves typing time — it does not replace your judgement. AI drafts always need your review before they go into the medical record. An AI scribe does not diagnose, does not give treatment advice, and does not calculate drug dosages. Your professional judgement, your medical expertise, and your review of every line are what make the record trustworthy. The tool changes where the time goes; the responsibility stays with you.
